## 6. Three ways through it

### Option A — designate existing cash as the opening reserve

The scheme holds **R210,844.15** with no stated purpose for it. Designating
**R60,000** as the opening reserve fund puts it above the 25% threshold
immediately, and the 15% floor stops applying. The annual contribution then
reverts to whatever the maintenance plan actually requires.

- **Effect on owners: none.** No levy increase. It is a book entry.
- **Effect on cash: none.** The reserve is notional; the money stays in the account.
- Leaves R150,844.15 as free administrative cash — still over six months' cover.

This is the obvious first move and it costs nobody anything. **Confirm with the
CSOS or the scheme's attorney that a designation of accumulated surplus satisfies
PMR 22** — the rule speaks of a budgeted contribution, and I am not qualified to
say whether a transfer from surplus counts.

### Option B — fund the floor from levies

Raise levies by **R483.25 per unit per month** on top of the 10.0% already in the
budget. That takes the total increase to roughly **30%**, which is a hard sell and
probably unnecessary given the cash on hand.

### Option C — reduce the water margin

Section 10 of the AGM report shows owners are billed about **R10,219 a year more
for water than the council charges**, most of it in the 20 kL common-property
provision against 6.94 kL of actual use. Redirecting that to the reserve funds a
quarter of the floor without anyone paying a rand more — but it is a
reallocation, not new money, and on its own it does not close the gap.

**The sensible combination is A now and C at the same meeting**, with the
maintenance plan completed during the year so that next year's contribution is
driven by the register rather than by a regulatory minimum.
